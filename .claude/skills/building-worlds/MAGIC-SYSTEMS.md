# Magic System Design

Comprehensive guide to designing magic systems, from hard to soft, incorporating Sanderson's Laws and principles from master fantasy writers.

---

## The Hard-Soft Spectrum

Magic systems exist on a spectrum from fully explained (hard) to mysterious (soft). Neither is superior; each serves different story needs.

### Hard Magic Systems

**Characteristics:**
- Clear, consistent rules readers understand
- Specific costs and limitations
- Magic can directly solve problems
- Often treated like a science or technology

**Strengths:**
- Satisfying problem-solving
- Fair play with readers
- Enables magic-based conflict
- Supports training/progression arcs

**Weaknesses:**
- Can feel mechanical
- Requires extensive planning
- Less wonder and mystery
- Rule-breaking feels like betrayal

**Examples:**
- Allomancy (Mistborn) - Metals grant specific powers
- Sympathy (Kingkiller) - Energy conservation laws
- Bending (Avatar) - Element-specific abilities

### Soft Magic Systems

**Characteristics:**
- Rules deliberately obscure or unstated
- Creates atmosphere and wonder
- Cannot reliably solve protagonist problems
- Often tied to mystery or the divine

**Strengths:**
- Preserves sense of wonder
- Flexible for storytelling
- Connects to mythic/spiritual themes
- Less front-loaded explanation

**Weaknesses:**
- Can't solve problems without feeling cheap
- Risk of deus ex machina
- Harder to create stakes
- Requires discipline to avoid convenience

**Examples:**
- Gandalf's Magic (LotR) - Capabilities unknown
- The Force (original trilogy) - Mystical, unexplained
- Earthsea True Names - Partial rules, much mystery

### Hybrid Systems

**The Best of Both:**
- Core rules are clear (hard)
- Higher levels remain mysterious (soft)
- Reader understands basics, marvels at mastery

**Examples:**
- Orogeny (Broken Earth) - Mechanics clear, full potential mysterious
- Stormlight (Sanderson) - Ten Surges explained, deeper cosmere mysteries remain

## Sanderson's Laws: Deep Application

### First Law: Understanding Equals Satisfaction

> "An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic."

**The Sliding Scale:**

| Reader Understanding | Problem-Solving Allowed |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Complete | Magic can be the solution |
| Partial | Magic can assist, not solve |
| Minimal | Magic can only complicate |
| None | Magic creates wonder only |

**Application Guide:**

**Before using magic to solve a problem, ask:**
1. Have I established this capability?
2. Does the reader know the costs?
3. Will this feel earned or cheap?

**The Gandalf Rule:** Gandalf's magic rarely solves problems because we don't understand its limits. When he acts, it's to create obstacles (lighting pinecones) or buy time (facing Balrog), not to solve the quest.

### Second Law: Limitations > Powers

> "Weaknesses, limits, and costs are more interesting than powers."

**The Three Types of Limitations:**

#### 1. Costs (What You Pay)

| Cost Type | Description | Story Effect |
|-----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Physical** | Exhaustion, injury, aging | Creates recovery time, vulnerability |
| **Mental** | Sanity, memory, personality | Long-term consequences, character change |
| **Material** | Rare components consumed | Economic stakes, scarcity plots |
| **Social** | Reputation, relationships | Isolation, moral dilemmas |
| **Temporal** | Time to cast, cooldowns | Tactical decisions, tension |
| **Moral** | Ethical compromises | Character corruption arcs |

#### 2. Weaknesses (What Hurts You)

| Weakness Type | Example | Story Function |
|--------------|---------|----------------|
| **Substance** | Kryptonite | Creates vulnerability |
| **Condition** | Sunlight kills vampires | Restricts when/where |
| **Countermeasure** | Specific wards block magic | Creates opposition |
| **Source Dependency** | Must touch ground | Creates tactical choices |

#### 3. Scope Limits (What You Can't Do)

| Limit Type | Example | Story Function |
|------------|---------|----------------|
| **Range** | Touch only | Forces proximity |
| **Scale** | One person at a time | Can't solve mass problems |
| **Target** | Only affects living things | Creates workarounds |
| **Duration** | Effects temporary | Time pressure |
| **Exclusion** | Can't affect self | Self-preservation stakes |

**Design Exercise:** For every power, define 3 limitations before use.

### Third Law: Expand Before Adding

> "Before adding something new to your magic, see if you can instead expand what you already have."

**The Depth Test:**

Ask these before adding a new magic type:
1. Can an existing power be combined or applied differently?
2. Can I explore an unexplored limitation?
3. Can I show mastery creating new applications?
4. Will this addition complicate or clarify?

**Signs You're Adding Too Much:**
- Readers can't track capabilities
- Powers exist for single scenes
- No power gets deep exploration
- Magic feels like a video game ability list

**Good Expansion Examples:**
- Basic fire magic → heating, light, steam power, metallurgy
- Telekinesis → fine control, self-flight, kinetic shields
- Healing → accelerating natural processes, biological knowledge

## Magic System Design Framework

### Step 1: Source

**Questions:**
- Where does magic come from?
- Is the source infinite or limited?
- Who can access it?
- Can access be gained or lost?

**Common Sources:**
| Source | Implications |
|--------|--------------|
| Innate/Genetic | Born special, chosen one tropes |
| Learned | Anyone can study, schools/masters |
| Divine | Tied to faith, granted by gods |
| Environmental | Location-based, natural forces |
| Technological | Can be mass-produced, industrialized |
| Sacrificial | Always has cost, dark implications |

### Step 2: Mechanics

**Questions:**
- How is magic activated? (Words, gestures, will, tools)
- What determines power level?
- How is it learned or improved?
- What does using magic feel like?

**Mechanical Choices:**
| Aspect | Options |
|--------|---------|
| **Activation** | Verbal, somatic, material, mental, tool-based |
| **Power Source** | Internal reserves, external energy, divine grant |
| **Scaling** | Practice improves, born at fixed level, tied to sacrifice |
| **Perception** | Visible effects, invisible, felt by sensitives |

### Step 3: Costs and Limitations

**Questions:**
- What does using magic cost?
- What can magic NOT do?
- What circumstances prevent use?
- What are the failure modes?

**Design the failure states as carefully as success states.**

### Step 4: Cultural Integration

**Questions:**
- How does society view magic users?
- How does magic affect the economy?
- How does magic affect warfare?
- How does magic affect daily life?
- What laws govern magic use?

**The Second-Order Effects:**

If healing magic exists:
- What happens to medicine?
- What happens to death?
- Who controls access?
- What are the economics?

If teleportation exists:
- What happens to trade routes?
- What happens to borders?
- What happens to crime?
- What happens to warfare?

**Every magical capability reshapes society.**

## Magic System Checklist

### Core Definition
- [ ] Source clearly defined
- [ ] Mechanics established
- [ ] At least 3 significant limitations
- [ ] Cost structure meaningful
- [ ] Failure modes described

### Internal Consistency
- [ ] Rules don't contradict
- [ ] Costs scale with power
- [ ] Exceptions are explained
- [ ] Same magic works same way

### Story Integration
- [ ] Magic serves theme
- [ ] Limitations create conflict
- [ ] Protagonist's magic has clear constraints
- [ ] Magic doesn't trivialize problems

### Cultural Impact
- [ ] Society reflects magic's existence
- [ ] Economic effects considered
- [ ] Political effects considered
- [ ] Daily life effects considered

### Reader Experience
- [ ] Rules introduceable gradually
- [ ] Can explain in one sentence (elevator pitch)
- [ ] Memorable and distinctive
- [ ] Visually/sensorily interesting

## The Rational Magic Sanity Check

**Before finalizing, ask:**

1. **The Conquest Question:** If this magic exists, why hasn't someone conquered the world with it?

2. **The Economy Question:** If this magic exists, why do people still do [manual labor X]?

3. **The Crime Question:** If this magic exists, what crimes are now possible and how are they prevented?

4. **The Healing Question:** If healing magic exists, how is death still meaningful?

5. **The Communication Question:** If instant communication exists, how do secrets exist?

**If you can't answer these, your magic system has holes.**

## Common Pitfalls

### The Power Creep Trap
**Problem:** Each book needs bigger magic
**Solution:** Depth, not height; explore existing magic more deeply

### The Deus Ex Machina Trap
**Problem:** New power appears to solve climax
**Solution:** Foreshadow capabilities, pay costs established earlier

### The Inconsistency Trap
**Problem:** Magic works differently each time
**Solution:** Document rules, review before writing magic scenes

### The Video Game Trap
**Problem:** Magic feels like ability lists
**Solution:** Focus on fewer powers with more implications

### The Meaningless Cost Trap
**Problem:** Costs exist but never matter
**Solution:** Force choices: using magic here means not using it there
