# The Five Consistency Pillars

These five pillars maintain reader trust and world believability. Violate them only intentionally and with explanation.

---

## Pillar 1: Magic Has Costs

**Rule:** Every use of power requires payment. No free lunch.

### Why This Matters
- Creates stakes and tension
- Forces meaningful choices
- Prevents magic from trivializing problems
- Makes success feel earned

### Cost Categories

| Category | Types | Story Function |
|----------|-------|----------------|
| **Physical** | Exhaustion, pain, injury, aging, death | Creates vulnerability windows |
| **Mental** | Sanity, memory, personality change, madness | Long-term character consequences |
| **Material** | Consumed components, rare ingredients | Economic stakes, scarcity plots |
| **Social** | Reputation, relationships, isolation | Character development, moral weight |
| **Temporal** | Time to cast, recovery periods, cooldowns | Tactical decisions, urgency |
| **Moral** | Ethical compromise, corruption, guilt | Theme exploration, character arc |

### The Cost Scaling Principle

Costs should scale with power:

| Power Level | Cost Level | Example |
|-------------|------------|---------|
| Minor | Negligible | Light spell → slight fatigue |
| Moderate | Noticeable | Healing wound → exhaustion, needs rest |
| Major | Significant | Resurrection → permanent sacrifice |
| World-changing | Extreme | Stopping war → death or worse |

### Consistency Check

Before any magic use, verify:
- [ ] Cost established previously or in-scene
- [ ] Cost proportional to effect
- [ ] Character actually pays the cost
- [ ] Cost creates real constraint (not just flavor)
- [ ] Same spell costs same each time

### Anti-Pattern: The Meaningless Cost

**Bad:**
```
She cast the healing spell and felt tired. Then she walked
five miles to the next town and fought three bandits.
```

**Good:**
```
She cast the healing spell. The world grayed at the edges.
When she woke, it was dark. Her legs wouldn't hold her.

"Can you walk?" he asked.

"Tomorrow. Maybe." She closed her eyes. The five miles
to town would have to wait.
```

## Pillar 2: Geography Matters

**Rule:** Distance, terrain, and environment are real constraints.

### Why This Matters
- Creates realistic logistics
- Enables meaningful journey narratives
- Shapes culture and politics
- Provides natural conflict sources

### The Distance Rule

Travel takes time. Be consistent.

| Travel Type | Speed (miles/day) | Considerations |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Walking | 15-25 | Terrain, weather, fitness |
| Horseback (sustained) | 25-35 | Horse care, rest stops |
| Forced march | 30-40 | Exhaustion, casualties |
| Ship (sail) | 50-100 | Wind, currents, coastline |
| Caravan | 10-15 | Slowest member, cargo |

**Calculate it:** If City A to City B is 300 miles, the journey is 10-20 days on foot, not "a few days later."

### Terrain Effects

| Terrain | Effect on Travel | Effect on Culture |
|---------|------------------|-------------------|
| Mountains | Slow, dangerous passes | Isolated valleys, defensible |
| Desert | Water limits distance | Oasis settlements, traders |
| Forest | Navigation difficult | Hidden communities, ambush |
| River | Highway OR barrier | Trade centers at crossings |
| Coast | Sea access | Fishing, naval power, trade |
| Plains | Fast travel | Hard to defend, pastoral culture |

### The Supply Question

Armies and large groups need:
- Food (people eat ~2lbs/day)
- Water (people drink ~1 gallon/day)
- Fodder for animals
- Shelter or camping gear
- Weapons maintenance

**The Campaign Constraint:** An army can only go where it can be fed. This shapes military history.

### Consistency Check

Before any journey or military campaign:
- [ ] Distance calculated reasonably
- [ ] Time passed matches distance
- [ ] Supplies addressed or explained
- [ ] Terrain affects the journey
- [ ] Weather mentioned if relevant
- [ ] Same route takes same time each time

### Anti-Pattern: The Teleporting Army

**Bad:**
```
"The enemy army crossed the mountains in three days."

[Reader notes: those same mountains took the heroes two weeks]
```

**Good:**
```
"The enemy army crossed the mountains in three days."

"Impossible. That's two weeks of march, minimum."

"They didn't march. They flew." He pointed upward. The dragons
had changed everything.
```

## Pillar 3: History Has Weight

**Rule:** Past events cast long shadows. Consequences persist.

### Why This Matters
- Makes world feel lived-in
- Provides motivation and conflict sources
- Creates depth without exposition
- Enables dramatic irony

### Types of Historical Persistence

| Category | Duration | Example |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Physical** | Centuries | Ruins, modified landscapes, monuments |
| **Political** | Generations | Borders, treaties, grudges, alliances |
| **Cultural** | Long-lasting | Traditions, holidays, naming patterns |
| **Personal** | Lifetimes | Family histories, inherited feuds |
| **Linguistic** | Very long | Place names, old words, idioms |

### The Ripple Effect Framework

Every major historical event creates ripples:

**Immediate (0-5 years):**
- Direct survivors' trauma/triumph
- Physical destruction/construction
- Political reorganization
- Economic disruption

**Medium-term (5-50 years):**
- Children raised with stories
- Monuments and memorials
- Laws changed in response
- Cultural attitudes shift

**Long-term (50+ years):**
- Event becomes legend/history
- Descendant identities form around it
- Physical evidence weathers
- Interpretations diverge

### The Scar Test

For any major historical event, identify:
1. **Physical scars:** What changed in the landscape?
2. **Political scars:** What borders/powers shifted?
3. **Cultural scars:** What taboos/traditions emerged?
4. **Personal scars:** What families were affected?

If you can't identify at least one, the event hasn't been integrated.

### Consistency Check

For historical events referenced:
- [ ] Timeline is consistent with other events
- [ ] Visible evidence exists (or explained why not)
- [ ] Cultural attitudes reflect the event
- [ ] Characters act consistent with their relationship to history
- [ ] Different groups remember differently (perspective)

### Anti-Pattern: The Forgotten Apocalypse

**Bad:**
```
"The Great War killed half the population twenty years ago."

[City appears fully populated, prosperous, no visible effects]
```

**Good:**
```
The city was half-empty, even now. Twenty years wasn't enough
to fill the houses the war had emptied. You could see it in
the architecture—old elegant buildings chopped into apartments,
workshops in what used to be ballrooms.

The children born after had a look. Hard. Like they'd learned
early that nothing was permanent.
```

## Pillar 4: Cultures Have Logic

**Rule:** Every custom, belief, and practice exists for a reason.

### Why This Matters
- Makes cultures feel real
- Enables organic world-building
- Prevents random "weirdness for weirdness"
- Creates deeper understanding

### The Origin Principle

Every cultural element has an origin. Common sources:

| Origin Type | Examples |
|-------------|----------|
| **Practical survival** | Don't eat poison fish → fish taboo |
| **Historical trauma** | Invasion → xenophobia, defensive architecture |
| **Resource scarcity** | Water shortage → sharing rituals, purity laws |
| **Religious revelation** | Prophet's vision → dietary laws, holidays |
| **Accident/coincidence** | King wore blue at victory → blue = good luck |
| **Borrowed/adapted** | Learned from neighbors, adapted to fit |

### The Logic Test

For any custom, you should be able to answer:
1. What need does this meet?
2. What conditions created it?
3. Why does it persist?
4. What would make it change?

**Note:** You don't need to explain this to readers. But you should know it.

### Internal Logic vs. External Logic

| Internal Logic | External Logic |
|----------------|----------------|
| Makes sense within the culture | Makes sense to outside observer |
| "We do this because ancestors did" | "They do this because environment X" |
| Characters believe this reason | Actual historical/practical cause |
| May include myth/religion | Grounded in cause and effect |

**Both should exist.** Characters have internal logic; the author knows external logic.

### Consistency Check

For cultural practices:
- [ ] Can explain origin (even if characters don't know it)
- [ ] Practice fits environment/history
- [ ] Characters treat it as normal (not exotic to themselves)
- [ ] Variations exist (regional, class, generational)
- [ ] Practice has consistent rules

### Anti-Pattern: The Inexplicable Taboo

**Bad:**
```
"The Northern people never touch copper. Nobody knows why.
It's just their weird custom."
```

**Good:**
```
"The Northern people never touch copper."

"Religious thing?"

"Practical. The copper in their rivers is toxic. Leaches into
anything copper touches. They learned the hard way a few
centuries back." She shrugged. "Now it's tradition. Younger
generation probably doesn't even know why anymore."
```

## Pillar 5: Power Has Limits

**Rule:** No character, faction, or magic is omnipotent. Everyone has constraints.

### Why This Matters
- Creates believable conflict
- Prevents "Why doesn't X just...?" questions
- Enables meaningful opposition
- Makes victories earned

### Types of Power Limits

| Limit Type | Description | Example |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| **Scope** | Can affect X but not Y | Fire mage can't affect water |
| **Scale** | Works on individuals not armies | Healing one person takes all day |
| **Knowledge** | Doesn't know how to do something | Ancient magic lost |
| **Permission** | Not allowed by rules/society/magic | Forbidden magic has consequences |
| **Resources** | Lacks materials/energy/people | Army needs food to march |
| **Opposition** | Others actively prevent | Enemy mages counter spells |
| **Personal** | Moral lines they won't cross | Won't kill children |
| **Time** | Takes too long | Ritual needs a month |

### The "Why Don't They Just..." Test

For every powerful entity, answer these:

1. **The Conquest Question:**
   "Why doesn't [powerful faction] just conquer everyone?"

2. **The Problem-Solving Question:**
   "Why doesn't [powerful character] just solve [main conflict]?"

3. **The Prevention Question:**
   "Why doesn't [powerful faction] just prevent [conflict]?"

If you can't answer, you have a power-limit problem.

### Power Limit Examples

| Powerful Entity | Limit Explanation |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Ancient wizard | Knowledge gaps; some things forgotten |
| Wealthy kingdom | Enemies would unite against aggression |
| All-seeing seer | Sees possibilities, not certainties; paralyzed by choice |
| Immortal elf | Patient to fault; moves too slowly |
| Powerful god | Bound by rules of divinity; mortals must choose |

### Consistency Check

For powerful characters/factions:
- [ ] Limits clearly established
- [ ] Limits are meaningful (not easily bypassed)
- [ ] Limits explain inaction on major problems
- [ ] Limits are consistent (don't disappear when inconvenient)
- [ ] Opponents have equivalent limits

### Anti-Pattern: The Inexplicably Inactive Power

**Bad:**
```
The wizard could destroy cities with a thought. He could read
minds, teleport anywhere, and raise the dead. But he didn't
help the heroes because he was "busy with other matters."
```

**Good:**
```
"Help us," she begged.

The wizard's eyes were kind. And sad. "Child, do you know what
I see when I look at the future? Every branch, every
possibility. Every good outcome you'd celebrate. Every horror
that follows. I know what happens if I intervene."

"Then why—"

"Because I also know what happens if I don't. And neither
future is good. The choice must be yours. That's the only
mercy I can offer. That the choice is yours."
```

## The Consistency Audit

Periodically review your world:

### Per Chapter/Scene
- [ ] Magic costs match established rules
- [ ] Travel time matches distance
- [ ] Characters act consistent with their history
- [ ] Cultural practices make sense

### Per Part/Arc
- [ ] Timeline remains consistent
- [ ] Power levels haven't crept
- [ ] Established rules haven't bent
- [ ] History is referenced appropriately

### Per Book
- [ ] All five pillars maintained
- [ ] No unexplained exceptions
- [ ] World feels coherent
- [ ] New elements build on established ones
