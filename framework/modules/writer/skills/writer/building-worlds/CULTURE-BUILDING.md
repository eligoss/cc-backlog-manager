# Culture Building

Guide to creating believable, internally consistent cultures using the iceberg principle, cultural pyramid, and anthropological frameworks from Le Guin, Jemisin, and world-building best practices.

---

## The Cultural Iceberg

Like an iceberg, most of a culture lies beneath the surface. Readers see 10%; you must know 90%.

### Above Water (10% - Show Directly)

What readers observe through characters:
- **Visual markers:** Clothing, architecture, art styles
- **Behavior:** Greetings, gestures, taboos in action
- **Language:** Dialect markers, unique terms, naming patterns
- **Food:** What they eat, how they eat, who eats together
- **Obvious structures:** Visible hierarchy, public rituals

### Below Water (90% - Imply, Don't Explain)

What you know but rarely state:
- **Historical origins:** Why customs exist
- **Deep beliefs:** Cosmology, afterlife, moral foundations
- **Economic foundations:** What creates and distributes wealth
- **Power structures:** Who really controls what
- **Psychological pressures:** Collective fears, aspirations
- **Family dynamics:** Inheritance, marriage, child-rearing
- **Unspoken rules:** What "everyone knows"

### The Iceberg in Practice

**Bad (exposing the iceberg):**
```
The Northerners wore blue because their ancestors believed ice
spirits protected them, and blue was the color of sacred ice.
This tradition dated back to the Frost Wars when...
```

**Good (showing the tip):**
```
She wore blue. Of course she did—everyone from the North did.
I'd stopped asking why years ago. The answer always involved
ice and spirits and a war nobody but them remembered.
```

## The Cultural Pyramid

Build cultures from the bottom up. Each layer emerges from the one below.

```
              /\
             /  \  5. EXPRESSION
            /    \    Art, stories, fashion
           /------\
          /        \  4. BELIEF SYSTEMS
         /          \    Religion, morality, cosmology
        /------------\
       /              \  3. SOCIAL ORGANIZATION
      /                \    Government, class, family
     /------------------\
    /                    \  2. SURVIVAL RESPONSES
   /                      \    Food, shelter, defense, health
  /--------------------------\
 /                            \  1. ENVIRONMENT
/                              \    Geography, climate, resources
--------------------------------
```

### Layer 1: Environment (Foundation)

Everything starts here. Geography shapes survival needs, which shape everything else.

**Questions:**
- What is the climate? (Temperature, seasons, precipitation)
- What natural resources exist? (Fertile land, minerals, timber, water)
- What natural dangers exist? (Predators, disasters, disease)
- What are the geographical constraints? (Mountains, rivers, coastlines)

**Environmental → Cultural Connections:**

| Environment | Cultural Effect |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Harsh winters | Value preparedness, community, storage |
| Abundant food | Leisure time, art development, population growth |
| Scarce water | Strict sharing rules, conflict over sources |
| Natural barriers | Isolation, distinct identity, insular attitudes |
| Trade routes | Cosmopolitan attitudes, merchant class, diversity |
| Dangerous wildlife | Warrior traditions, protective architecture |

### Layer 2: Survival Responses

How the culture meets basic needs given its environment.

**Food:**
- What do they eat? (Shapes cuisine, festivals, trade)
- How do they get it? (Farming, herding, hunting, trade)
- Who controls food? (Power structures)
- What foods are taboo? (Usually practical origin)

**Shelter:**
- What materials are available?
- How do structures handle climate?
- Who lives together? (Family structure)
- Who builds? (Specialization, class)

**Defense:**
- What are the threats?
- How is defense organized? (Military, militia, walls)
- Who fights? (Warrior class, all citizens, hired)
- How does defense shape government?

**Health:**
- What diseases are common?
- What remedies exist? (Herbalism, magic, surgery)
- How is death handled?
- What creates "cleanliness" concepts?

### Layer 3: Social Organization

How the culture structures itself given survival needs.

**Power Structures:**
- Who rules? (Hereditary, elected, strongest, wisest, richest)
- Why do they rule? (Divine right, conquest, competence, tradition)
- What limits their power? (Laws, councils, rebellion threat)
- How is succession handled?

**Class and Hierarchy:**
- What classes exist?
- How is class determined? (Birth, wealth, ability, achievement)
- Can people change class?
- What privileges/restrictions apply?

**Family Structure:**
- What defines family? (Nuclear, extended, clan, chosen)
- Who heads the family?
- How are marriages arranged?
- How is property inherited?

**Gender Roles:**
- What roles are gendered?
- Why? (Often practical origin, then tradition)
- How rigid are the boundaries?
- What happens to those who don't fit?

### Layer 4: Belief Systems

How the culture makes meaning of existence.

**Cosmology:**
- How was the world created?
- What forces govern reality?
- What exists beyond death?
- What is the purpose of existence?

**Morality:**
- What is good? What is evil?
- How are these concepts taught?
- What punishes wrongdoing? (Gods, society, karma)
- What rewards virtue?

**Religion:**
- Monotheist, polytheist, animist, or other?
- How organized is religious practice?
- What is the clergy's role?
- How does religion interact with power?

**Magic's Place:**
- Is magic divine, natural, or suspicious?
- Who can practice it?
- What ethical rules govern it?
- How does it fit the cosmology?

### Layer 5: Expression

The visible products of deeper cultural layers.

**Art and Aesthetics:**
- What is considered beautiful?
- What art forms exist?
- Who creates art? (Specialists, everyone)
- What purposes does art serve?

**Stories and Literature:**
- What stories do they tell?
- What heroes do they celebrate?
- What cautionary tales exist?
- How is history remembered?

**Music and Performance:**
- What instruments exist?
- What occasions involve music?
- Who performs?
- What is sacred vs. secular?

**Fashion and Decoration:**
- What do people wear?
- What do the clothes signify?
- What body modifications exist?
- What displays status?

## The Three-Difference Rule

Every culture needs at least three significant differences from "default fantasy medieval Europe" or whatever your baseline is.

**Categories to differentiate:**

1. **Relationship with magic/technology**
   - Who uses it? How is it viewed? Who controls it?

2. **Social structure distinctive element**
   - Unusual family structure, gender roles, class system, or governance

3. **Historical defining moment**
   - A trauma or triumph that shaped current attitudes

**Example:**
- Default: Medieval European kingdom
- Difference 1: Magic is illegal (creates underground, conflict with neighbors)
- Difference 2: Inheritance through youngest child (different family dynamics)
- Difference 3: Nearly destroyed by plague, now obsessed with purity (taboos, hygiene, isolation)

## Avoiding the Monoculture

Real societies have internal variation. Fantasy cultures shouldn't be monolithic.

**Sources of Internal Variation:**

| Dimension | Creates Variation In |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Region** | Dialect, cuisine, local customs, local heroes |
| **Class** | Education, manners, dress, opportunity, concerns |
| **Urban vs. Rural** | Cosmopolitanism, tradition, lifestyle, occupation |
| **Generation** | Values, attitudes toward change, cultural references |
| **Religion** | Moral frameworks, holidays, dietary rules |
| **Occupation** | Worldview, vocabulary, daily rhythm |

**The Variation Test:** If you can describe an entire nation in one paragraph with no exceptions, it's too uniform.

## Culture Design Checklist

### Foundation (Required)
- [ ] Environment clearly defined
- [ ] Food sources and cuisine sketched
- [ ] Survival challenges identified
- [ ] Basic governance structure

### Structure (Important)
- [ ] Class system exists or explicitly absent
- [ ] Family structure defined
- [ ] Gender roles addressed (even if equal)
- [ ] Religion or equivalent belief system

### Distinctiveness (Essential)
- [ ] Three significant differences from baseline
- [ ] At least one internal variation source
- [ ] Unique naming conventions
- [ ] Distinctive visual markers

### Depth Check (Quality)
- [ ] Can explain why customs exist
- [ ] Environment → culture connections clear
- [ ] No random inexplicable taboos
- [ ] Culture affects character behavior

## Culture Design Shortcuts

When you need a culture quickly:

### The Environment-First Method
1. Define climate and geography
2. Ask: What would people eat, wear, build?
3. Ask: What would they fear?
4. Ask: Who would have power?
5. Add one distinctive twist

### The History-First Method
1. Define one world-changing event
2. Ask: How would people respond?
3. Ask: What would they value afterward?
4. Ask: What would they fear?
5. Build backward to environment

### The Contrast Method
1. Take an existing culture from your world
2. Define what they believe
3. Create a culture that believes the opposite
4. Figure out why both make sense

## Cultural Interaction

When cultures meet:

**Contact Types:**
| Type | Result |
|------|--------|
| Trade | Cultural exchange, pidgin languages, hybrid customs |
| War | Hatred, stereotypes, boundary definition |
| Migration | Diaspora cultures, assimilation pressure, hybrid identities |
| Conquest | Dominant/subordinate dynamics, resistance, cultural suppression |

**Conflict Sources:**
- Different values appearing as insults
- Competition for resources
- Historical grievances
- Religious incompatibility
- Economic competition

## Integration with Magic Systems

Magic transforms culture. Consider:

**If magic is common:**
- Economy includes magical services
- Laws govern magical use
- Education includes magic training
- Architecture incorporates magical defenses

**If magic is rare:**
- Magic users are special (revered or feared)
- Non-magical solutions dominate
- Magic users form separate social class
- Access to magic is political power

**If magic is new:**
- Society is in transition
- Institutions struggling to adapt
- Generational conflict about magic
- Rapid social change

**If magic is lost:**
- Legends of past greatness
- Artifacts without understanding
- Superstition replacing knowledge
- Quest to restore magic
