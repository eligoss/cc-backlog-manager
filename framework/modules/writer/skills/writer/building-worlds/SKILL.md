# Skill: Building Worlds

**Purpose:** Design coherent fantasy worlds with internally consistent magic, cultures, geography, and history.

**Version:** 1.0.0

**Scope:** Framework-level (project-agnostic)

---

## When to Use This Skill

- Designing or refining magic systems
- Creating cultures and societies
- Building historical timelines
- Designing geography and environment
- Ensuring world consistency across a series
- Planning information revelation to readers

**Do NOT use for:**
- File organization (use `organizing-books`)
- Prose style and writing craft (use `writing-prose`)
- Character development (separate concern)

---

## Core Philosophy

### The Three Pillars of World-Building

| Pillar | Source | Principle |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| **Depth Over Density** | Sanderson | Expand what exists before adding new elements |
| **Internal Logic** | Tolkien | Every element connects to world's core truth |
| **Grounded Fantasy** | Jemisin | Anchor magic in something tangible and real |

### The Master World-Builders

**Brandon Sanderson** - The Architect
- Hard magic systems with clear rules
- Three Laws of Magic
- "Worldbuilder's disease" prevention
- Limitations create story, not powers

**J.R.R. Tolkien** - The Subcreator
- Internal consistency as primary virtue
- Language shapes culture shapes history
- Myth and legend as world memory
- Depth through implication, not exposition

**N.K. Jemisin** - The Grounder
- Anchor magic in tangible concepts (geology, physics)
- Oppression systems with real-world logic
- Magic costs reflect real sacrifices
- Theme and world are inseparable

**Ursula K. Le Guin** - The Anthropologist
- Cultures as complete systems
- Economics shapes society
- Maps reveal world logic
- Every custom has origin

**George R.R. Martin** - The Gardener
- Discover world through writing
- Consequences as world-building
- Political complexity
- Character-driven revelation

## The Architect-Gardener Spectrum

| Approach | Best For | Risk |
|----------|----------|------|
| **Pure Architect** | Hard magic, mystery plots | Sterile, over-planned |
| **Hybrid** (Recommended) | Most fantasy | Requires discipline |
| **Pure Gardener** | Character-driven stories | Inconsistency, retcons |

**Hybrid Approach:**
1. Plan foundation (magic rules, core history, geography)
2. Discover details through writing (customs, minor characters)
3. Lock rules once established (use immutability system)

## The World-Building Pyramid

Build from bottom to top. Each layer depends on those below.

```
        /\
       /  \  POLISH
      /    \  Minor customs, regional variations
     /------\
    /        \  DETAIL
   /          \  Cultures, economics, religion
  /------------\
 /              \  STRUCTURE
/                \  Geography, major history, power systems
------------------
    FOUNDATION
    Core premise, magic rules, physical laws
```

### Layer 1: Foundation (Establish First)

**Must define before writing:**
- Core world premise/conflict
- Magic system rules (if magic exists)
- Physical laws that differ from reality
- What makes this world unique

**Questions to answer:**
- What is the one thing readers must understand?
- What rules can never be broken?
- What distinguishes this from generic fantasy?

### Layer 2: Structure (Build on Foundation)

**Define early, refine as needed:**
- Continental geography and climate
- Major historical events (world-shaping)
- Power structures (who rules, why)
- Major factions and their relationships

### Layer 3: Detail (Fill in as Needed)

**Develop when story requires:**
- Specific cultures and customs
- Economic systems and trade
- Religious beliefs and practices
- Daily life details

### Layer 4: Polish (Discover Through Writing)

**Let these emerge organically:**
- Minor customs and superstitions
- Regional dialect variations
- Character-specific knowledge
- Small-scale traditions

## Sanderson's Three Laws of Magic

### First Law: Understanding = Satisfaction

> "An author's ability to solve conflict with magic is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic."

| Magic Type | Reader Understanding | Problem-Solving Role |
|------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| **Hard Magic** | High (rules clear) | Can solve problems directly |
| **Soft Magic** | Low (mysterious) | Creates wonder, complicates problems |
| **Hybrid** | Partial | Strategic use of both |

**Implication:** If magic solves the climax, readers must understand how it works beforehand.

### Second Law: Limitations > Powers

> "Weaknesses, limits, and costs are more interesting than powers."

**Types of Limitations:**
- **Costs:** What you pay to use magic (energy, health, sanity, materials)
- **Weaknesses:** What makes you vulnerable (kryptonite-style)
- **Scope Limits:** What magic cannot affect

**The Superman Test:** What makes Superman interesting?
1. His code of ethics (self-imposed limit)
2. Kryptonite (external weakness)

Without limits, there's no story.

### Third Law: Expand Before Adding

> "Before adding something new to your magic, see if you can expand what you already have."

**Depth beats breadth.** One well-developed power system is more satisfying than ten shallow ones.

**Signs of Worldbuilder's Disease:**
- Adding new magic types to solve each problem
- Endless list of abilities with no depth
- Readers can't track what's possible

## The Five Consistency Pillars

Maintain these for reader trust. See [CONSISTENCY-PILLARS.md](./CONSISTENCY-PILLARS.md) for details.

| Pillar | Rule | Violation Example |
|--------|------|-------------------|
| **Magic Has Costs** | Power requires sacrifice | Free healing with no exhaustion |
| **Geography Matters** | Distance and terrain are real | Three-week journey in one day |
| **History Has Weight** | Past shapes present visibly | War 20 years ago, no consequences |
| **Cultures Have Logic** | Every custom has origin | Random taboos with no reason |
| **Power Has Limits** | No one is omnipotent | Wizard who could solve everything but doesn't |

## The Iceberg Principle

**Show 10%, imply 90%.** The world should feel deeper than what's on the page.

### Above Water (What Readers See)
- Current events and immediate conflicts
- Physical details of present locations
- Active characters and relationships
- Surface-level cultural practices

### Below Water (What You Know)
- Deep history beyond what's relevant
- Distant lands and their politics
- Complete magical theory
- Economic systems and trade networks
- Religious doctrine and philosophy

**Key insight:** You must know the 90% to write the 10% convincingly, but you must NOT put the 90% on the page.

## Quick Decision Tables

### Magic System Type Selection

| Story Need | Choose | Why |
|------------|--------|-----|
| Protagonist solves problem with magic | Hard | Reader must understand rules |
| Magic creates atmosphere/wonder | Soft | Mystery enhances immersion |
| Magic-based conflict/competition | Hard | Clear stakes require clear rules |
| Magic as metaphor/theme | Soft | Flexibility serves meaning |
| Training/progression arc | Hard | Measurable growth |

### Culture Complexity Levels

| Story Role | Depth Needed | Development Time |
|------------|--------------|------------------|
| Protagonist's home | Deep | Full pyramid |
| Major faction | Medium | Layers 1-3 |
| Visited briefly | Surface | Layers 1-2 |
| Mentioned only | Minimal | One distinct trait |

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### Worldbuilder's Disease
**Symptom:** Endless planning, no writing
**Cure:** Stop when you have enough to start; discover the rest

### The Info-Dump
**Symptom:** Paragraphs of history/exposition
**Cure:** Reveal through action, dialogue, discovery

### The Monoculture
**Symptom:** Entire nation has one personality
**Cure:** Add regional, class, generational variation

### The Convenient Exception
**Symptom:** Rules break when plot needs it
**Cure:** Lock rules; rewrite plot instead

### The Kitchen Sink
**Symptom:** Every fantasy creature/system included
**Cure:** Third Law - expand before adding
