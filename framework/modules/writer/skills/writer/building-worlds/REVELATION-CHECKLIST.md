# World Revelation Checklist

How to reveal your world to readers: pacing, methods, and anti-patterns.

---

## The Revelation Principle

**You know 100% of your world. Readers should learn about 10-30%, revealed gradually.**

The goal is not to communicate everything you know, but to create the feeling of a deep, real world while serving the story.

---

## Revelation Timing by Story Phase

### First Third: Establish Mystery

**Goal:** Show the world has depth. Create questions.

| Technique | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Partial information | References to events without explanation |
| Contradictory accounts | Two characters remember differently |
| Unexplained artifacts | Objects with obvious but unstated significance |
| Hinted history | "Before the Fall..." without explaining the Fall |
| Assumed knowledge | Characters don't explain what "everyone knows" |

**Reader experience:** "This world has secrets. I want to know more."

### Second Third: Connect Patterns

**Goal:** Reward attention. Answer some questions. Create new ones.

| Technique | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Convergent information | Two sources confirm same fact |
| Revealed connections | "The Fall" links to current conflict |
| Deeper implications | What seemed simple is complex |
| Perspective expansion | Enemy's viewpoint reveals hidden logic |
| Partial answers | One mystery solved, revealing larger mystery |

**Reader experience:** "Things are coming together. But there's more."

### Final Third: Deliver Payoff

**Goal:** Satisfy curiosity. Resolve major mysteries. Leave some for sequels.

| Technique | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| Direct revelation | Character finally explains |
| Assembled picture | Reader puts pieces together |
| Climactic discovery | Plot and revelation merge |
| Recontextualization | Known facts gain new meaning |
| Strategic mystery preservation | Some questions remain |

**Reader experience:** "That's what it all meant. But what about...?"

## Revelation Methods

### Method 1: Through Character Discovery

Character learns; reader learns with them.

**Best for:**
- Naive protagonists
- Mystery-driven plots
- Audience surrogate characters

**Risk:** Info-dumping through "mentor explains"

### Method 2: Through Conflict

Information emerges because characters need it.

**Best for:**
- Action-heavy narratives
- Urgent pacing
- Avoiding exposition scenes

**Risk:** Feeling forced or convenient

### Method 3: Through Environment

World details visible in setting.

**Best for:**
- Visual world-building
- Subtle revelation
- Showing not telling

**Risk:** Reader missing important details

### Method 4: Through Dialogue

Characters discuss naturally.

**Best for:**
- Cultural information
- Character perspectives
- Debate and nuance

**Risk:** "As you know, Bob..." syndrome

### Method 5: Through Artifacts

Objects tell stories.

**Best for:**
- Historical information
- Mystery creation
- Non-verbal revelation

**Risk:** Heavy-handed symbolism

## The Information Hierarchy

**What to reveal, in order of priority:**

1. **Need to know NOW** - Information required for current scene to make sense
2. **Need to know SOON** - Information required for upcoming plot
3. **Nice to know** - Enriches understanding, not essential
4. **Can wonder about** - Intentional mysteries
5. **Never need to know** - Your notes only

**Rule:** Only reveal from categories 1-2. Sprinkle 3. Preserve 4. Keep 5 hidden.

## Revelation Anti-Patterns

### The Info-Dump

**Symptom:** Paragraphs of history/explanation
**Cure:** Break into small pieces across scenes

**Bad:**
```
The War of Shadows was fought 300 years ago between the
Northern Alliance and the Southern Empire. It lasted
forty years and killed millions. The Northern Alliance
won because of their superior magic, which was developed
by the Archmage Valdris who discovered the principle of
sympathetic resonance in the crystalline matrices...
```

**Good:**
```
"Nobody crosses the Ashfields."
"Why?"
"The war." He spat. "Three centuries dead and the land
still won't grow. Magic poisoned it."
"Which war?"
He looked at her like she'd asked which direction was up.
"The only one that matters."
```

### The "As You Know" Dialogue

**Symptom:** Characters explain things they both already know
**Cure:** Only explain to characters who don't know

**Bad:**
```
"As you know, Father, our family has held this castle for
twelve generations since the founding of the kingdom."
```

**Good:**
```
"Twelve generations," her father said, looking at the walls.
"And now this."
She didn't answer. What was there to say?
```

### The Tour Guide

**Symptom:** Character explains every sight
**Cure:** Let reader observe without commentary

**Bad:**
```
"That's the Temple of the Sun God, built five hundred years
ago. Over there is the marketplace where merchants from
across the continent come to trade. And that statue
commemorates King Aldric the Bold who..."
```

**Good:**
```
The temple dominated the square. Old. Five centuries at least,
from the architectural style. The market sprawled around it,
foreign tongues mixing with local shouts. At the center, a
bronze king watched it all with blind eyes.
```

### The Convenient Expert

**Symptom:** Perfect source appears exactly when needed
**Cure:** Establish sources before needed, or work for information

**Bad:**
```
"We need to know about ancient dragon magic."
A man in the corner stood. "I happen to be the world's
foremost expert on ancient dragon magic."
```

**Good:**
```
"We need to know about ancient dragon magic."
"There's one person who might know. The hermit in the
Eastern Waste. She was there when the last dragon died."
"The Eastern Waste is three weeks of travel."
"Then we'd better start walking."
```

## Revelation Planning Template

For major world elements, plan revelation:

```
ELEMENT: [Magic system / Historical event / Cultural fact]

Story need: [Why readers must know this]

REVELATION PLAN:
- First third: [What hints/fragments]
- Second third: [What connections/partial reveals]
- Final third: [What full explanations]

METHOD: [Character discovery / Conflict / Environment / Dialogue / Artifact]

WHAT STAYS HIDDEN: [What readers never fully learn]
```

## Quick Checklist

### Before Writing a Scene

- [ ] What must readers know for this scene to work?
- [ ] What can readers wonder about after this scene?
- [ ] Is there any info-dumping to cut?
- [ ] Does any "as you know" dialogue exist?

### After Drafting

- [ ] Could any exposition be shown instead of told?
- [ ] Is information revealed at the moment of maximum impact?
- [ ] Do mysteries created earlier get addressed?
- [ ] Is the pace of revelation appropriate (not too fast/slow)?

### Per Book

- [ ] Major mysteries established in first third
- [ ] Connections forming in second third
- [ ] Satisfying reveals in final third
- [ ] Some mystery preserved for future

---

## The Ultimate Test

**If readers feel the world is shallow:** You're not implying enough below-surface depth.

**If readers feel confused:** You're not revealing enough surface information.

**If readers feel lectured:** You're revealing too much too directly.

**The sweet spot:** Readers understand enough to follow the story while sensing vastly more beneath.
