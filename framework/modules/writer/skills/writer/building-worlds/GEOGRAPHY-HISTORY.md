# Geography and History Design

Physical world construction and timeline development. Geography shapes history; history marks geography. They are inseparable.

---

## Part I: Geography Design

### The Geography-First Principle

Geography determines:
- Where people settle
- What resources they access
- Who they trade with
- Who they fight
- What they eat, wear, build
- What they believe

**Start with geography. Everything else follows.**

### Core Geographic Elements

#### 1. Climate Zones

| Zone | Characteristics | Cultural Tendency |
|------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Tropical** | Hot, wet, dense vegetation | Riverine civilization, disease challenges |
| **Temperate** | Moderate seasons, varied terrain | Agricultural heartlands, population centers |
| **Arid/Desert** | Extreme heat, minimal water | Oasis settlements, nomadic cultures |
| **Mediterranean** | Dry summer, wet winter | Maritime trade, olive/wine cultures |
| **Continental** | Extreme seasons | Hardy peoples, storage-focused |
| **Arctic/Tundra** | Perpetual cold | Small populations, survival focus |

#### 2. Terrain Features

| Feature | Strategic Value | Cultural Impact |
|---------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Mountains** | Defense, mineral wealth | Isolated communities, mining culture |
| **Rivers** | Trade, irrigation, transport | Cities at confluences, boundaries |
| **Forests** | Resources, concealment | Logging, independent communities |
| **Plains** | Agriculture, movement | Large kingdoms, cavalry cultures |
| **Coasts** | Trade, fishing, naval power | Maritime empires, port cities |
| **Islands** | Natural defense, isolation | Unique cultures, naval focus |
| **Deserts** | Barriers, resource scarcity | Trade routes matter intensely |
| **Swamps** | Defense, resources, danger | Marginalized or mystical peoples |

#### 3. Resource Distribution

Resources create trade, conflict, and power.

| Resource | Who Controls It | Who Needs It | Conflict Potential |
|----------|-----------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Fertile land | Farmers, lords | Everyone | Very high |
| Fresh water | Upstream nations | Downstream nations | High |
| Minerals/metals | Mining cultures | Manufacturing cultures | Moderate-high |
| Timber | Forest nations | Plains/desert nations | Moderate |
| Strategic positions | Who holds them | Anyone wanting passage | Very high |
| Magical resources | Varies | Magic users | Depends on magic system |

### The Settlement Logic

People settle where they can survive and thrive.

**Primary settlement factors:**
1. Water access (absolute requirement)
2. Food production potential
3. Defense capability
4. Trade route access
5. Resource availability

**City placement checklist:**
- [ ] Water source identified
- [ ] Food supply explained (farmland, fishing, trade)
- [ ] Defense advantages noted (if applicable)
- [ ] Trade connections plausible
- [ ] Growth explained by above factors

### The Natural Border Principle

Borders follow geography before politics.

| Feature | Border Stability |
|---------|-----------------|
| Mountain ranges | Very stable, clear |
| Major rivers | Stable, but crossable |
| Deserts | Stable barriers |
| Forests | Fuzzy, disputed |
| Plains | Unstable, shift with power |
| Coastlines | Clear for maritime borders |

**Unnatural borders** (straight lines, arbitrary divisions) suggest:
- Recent conquest
- Colonial imposition
- Magical boundary
- Future conflict

### Map Logic Checklist

- [ ] Rivers flow downhill to seas/lakes
- [ ] Mountains form ranges, not random peaks
- [ ] Deserts have logical placement (rain shadows, latitudes)
- [ ] Cities are at water sources, trade intersections, or defensive positions
- [ ] Borders follow geographic features where stable
- [ ] Climate zones make sense for latitude and terrain

## Part II: History Design

### The Timeline Backbone

History provides depth without requiring detailed knowledge for every reader.

**Timeline layers:**

| Layer | What It Contains | Reader Knowledge |
|-------|------------------|------------------|
| **Mythic** | Creation, gods, first peoples | Legends, disputed |
| **Ancient** | Fallen empires, lost knowledge | Ruins, artifacts |
| **Historical** | Documented past, verifiable | Records, memories |
| **Recent** | Living memory | Characters recall |
| **Current** | Story present | Directly shown |

### Designing Eras

Each era needs:
1. **Name** (what people call it)
2. **Defining characteristics** (what made it unique)
3. **Major events** (what shaped it)
4. **Transition** (how it ended)
5. **Legacy** (what it left behind)

**Era design template:**
```
ERA: [Name]
Duration: [Approximate years]
Defined by: [1-2 key characteristics]
Major events: [3-5 significant occurrences]
Ended by: [Transition event]
Legacy: [What remains - ruins, traditions, artifacts, attitudes]
```

### Event Design

Not all historical events are equal. Categorize by impact:

| Impact Level | Scope | Examples | Persistence |
|--------------|-------|----------|-------------|
| **World-changing** | Entire world | Creation of magic, god's death | Millennia |
| **Civilization-defining** | Multiple nations | Great wars, plagues, golden ages | Centuries |
| **Nation-shaping** | Single nation | Founding, conquest, revolution | Generations |
| **Local significance** | Region/city | Battles, disasters, heroes | Decades |
| **Personal relevance** | Individuals | Character backstory events | Lifetimes |

### The Consequence Web

Every significant event creates consequences. Map them:

```
                    [EVENT]
                       |
         +-------------+-------------+
         |             |             |
    [IMMEDIATE]   [MEDIUM-TERM]  [LONG-TERM]
         |             |             |
    +----+----+   +----+----+   +----+----+
    |    |    |   |    |    |   |    |    |
  [Political] [Economic] [Cultural] [Physical]
```

**Example: Major War**
- Immediate: Deaths, destruction, power shift
- Medium-term: Orphan generation, economic rebuilding, new borders
- Long-term: Grudges, monuments, cultural memory, changed demographics

### Historical Conflicts

History is shaped by conflict. Types:

| Conflict Type | Drivers | Resolution Methods |
|---------------|---------|-------------------|
| **Resource** | Scarcity, control | Trade, conquest, innovation |
| **Territory** | Expansion, defense | War, treaty, marriage |
| **Succession** | Power vacuum, ambition | Civil war, compromise, assassination |
| **Religious** | Belief incompatibility | Conversion, separation, war |
| **Cultural** | Values clash | Assimilation, isolation, conflict |
| **Economic** | Trade disputes, debt | Negotiation, sanctions, war |

### The Living History Principle

History affects the present. For each major historical event, identify:

1. **What characters know about it**
2. **What characters feel about it**
3. **What physical evidence remains**
4. **How it shapes current conflicts**
5. **Who benefits from which interpretation**

### Timeline Consistency

Common timeline errors to avoid:

| Error | Example | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|
| **Impossible lifespans** | Character present at events 200 years apart | Check ages, use longevity explanation |
| **Technology anachronism** | Advanced tech before required developments | Trace tech development |
| **Missing recovery time** | Kingdom fully recovered 5 years after devastation | Add realistic recovery periods |
| **Forgotten events** | Civilization-ending event unknown to characters | Explain knowledge loss or acknowledge |
| **Contradictory sequences** | Event A caused B, but B happened first | Map cause-effect carefully |

## Part III: Geography-History Integration

### How Geography Shapes History

| Geographic Factor | Historical Effect |
|-------------------|-------------------|
| Mountain ranges | Political divisions, cultural isolation, defensive wars |
| River valleys | Cradles of civilization, trade routes, flood-driven cultures |
| Natural resources | Wealth concentration, conflict zones, specialized cultures |
| Climate shifts | Migration, collapse, adaptation |
| Natural disasters | Founding myths, cultural trauma, population shifts |
| Strategic locations | Repeated conquests, cosmopolitan cultures, military focus |

### How History Marks Geography

| Historical Event | Geographic Evidence |
|------------------|---------------------|
| Wars | Battlefields, destroyed cities, defensive structures |
| Civilizations | Ruins, modified landscapes, road systems |
| Migrations | Place names, cultural layers, genetic diversity |
| Natural disasters | Changed coastlines, absent forests, new terrain |
| Magic events | Impossible geography, cursed lands, blessed regions |

### The Integration Checklist

- [ ] Major geographic features have historical significance
- [ ] Major historical events left geographic marks
- [ ] Current borders reflect both geography and history
- [ ] Culture reflects both environment and past
- [ ] Trade routes follow geographic logic and historical development
- [ ] Cities exist for combined geographic and historical reasons

---

## Quick Reference: Design Order

**Recommended sequence:**

1. **Sketch geography** (continents, major features)
2. **Place climates** (temperature zones, rain patterns)
3. **Add resources** (farmland, minerals, water)
4. **Identify natural borders** (mountains, rivers, coasts)
5. **Place initial settlements** (water + food + defense)
6. **Design mythic era** (world creation, first peoples)
7. **Create ancient civilizations** (now fallen, leave ruins)
8. **Develop recent history** (current nations, conflicts)
9. **Connect to story present** (what characters know)
